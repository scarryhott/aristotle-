# Metaphysical Basis: Relative Axiom-Geometry and Natural Translational Existence

## Foundational claim

The foundational claim is not merely that everything is related, nor merely that translations preserve already-defined mathematical objects. Its central question is:

> **Does there exist a translation independent of the selection of an origin language, whose relations themselves form reference frames, and can such translation relate formal identities across changing frames?**

The thesis proposes that **axioms are relative to their geometries/reference frames and translation is foundational to the natural existence available between such frames**. A reference frame is therefore not metaphysically prior to relation: local frames are formations within an origin-independent translational relation.

More strongly, translation is understood here in a relational sense prior to
the distinction between a fixed language, definition, institution, or local
topos. Those structures are local appearances of a translational relation, not
its absolute source. The present formal kernel does not yet begin at that prior
level; its required formal openings are recorded in
[Pre-institutional translation: formal openings](PREINSTITUTIONAL_TRANSLATION_OPENINGS.md).

```text
origin-independent translational relation
        ↓
relative relational formations / reference frames
        ↓
axiom_F ↔ geometry_F ↔ admitted equality_F
        ↓
relational definition
        ↓
translational completion / closure
```

The project does not require one absolute axiom system, geometry, language, or selected origin from which all others inherit identity. A local mathematical frame is a unified axiom-geometric formation. Another frame `G` may have a different form. Neither must first be reduced to a privileged third frame. Their common identity, when available, is disclosed relationally through admissible translation and return.

This is the claimed foundational novelty. Groupoid coherence, quotients, natural transformations, sections, universal properties, finite runtime certificates, and other classical constructions are downstream mathematical forms used to express and test consequences of this starting point.

## Relation forms frames; frames do not freeze axiometry

“Relations form frames” does not mean that one completed relation permanently freezes an axiom system. A frame is a relative formation in an evolving translational structure. Its local axiometry can change as new relations, translations, returns, and completions alter the available perspective.

Accordingly, the experimental runtime may **freeze an evidence artifact** before a comparison so that the experiment cannot retrospectively rewrite it, but this causal freeze is not a metaphysical claim that axiometry itself is static.

The intended distinction is:

```text
experimental freeze = preserve what a particular episode actually produced
metaphysical closure = allow perspective/axiometry to evolve through auditable translation and return
```

Closure is therefore **interactive continual completion**: relational formations can become new frames and new bases for subsequent translation rather than terminating in one final canonical language.

## Natural existence

“Natural” is meant literally as well as metaphysically. In the formal translational frame, what persists across languages satisfies the commuting return relation

```text
W_m(T_lm u) = phi_lm(W_l u).
```

The interpretation is not that `F` and `G` are absolutely identified before this square is checked. The square expresses the recoverable identity available **through** their translation. Translation is therefore not merely transport of a prior absolute definition; it participates in constituting cross-frame relational identity.

A reference frame remains usable without becoming an origin. Any frame may locally represent returned identity; changing frames changes the presentation while coherent translation preserves the relational content available between them.

### Choice, cost, and non-circular naturality

The metaphysical reading must not turn naturality into a hidden global chooser.
Translation is the shared language within which local formations can make
interactive choices; a proposed return, orientation, extension, or
identification is first recorded, and only then tested for commutation and
recovery. Naturality is therefore a constraint on a choice, not the mechanism
that forces the choice to exist. The current runtime records this as explicit
obligations and counterexamples, not as a scalar cost functional.

relative asymmetry, local/global topology, and Turing/topos bridge boundary.

## Relative axiom and geometry

An axiom is not metaphysically prior to geometry, nor geometry prior to axiom. Their role is relative and mutually disclosed inside a selected frame. A genuinely novel axiom-geometry must therefore first be admitted conditionally in its own unified manner before comparison with another frame.

To evaluate a novel frame `G` does not mean assuming that `G` is absolutely true. It means:

```text
if G is the current reference frame,
evaluate its equality and consequences under G;
then ask how G relates translationally to F;
allow completed relational return to participate in the next frame/basis.
```

This avoids treating an older frame `F` as a supposedly neutral metalanguage while also avoiding the opposite error of freezing `G` as a final origin.

## Equality, relational definition, and openness

The frame-conditional layer represents a reference frame by its admitted equality. A comparison becomes `GeomEquiv` when it preserves and reflects the equalities of the two frames:

```text
x ~_F y  ↔  T(x) ~_G T(y).
```

Resolution is relational factorization through that equality. A total question is open in a frame exactly when it separates occurrences that the frame itself equates. Openness is therefore not missing computation, non-selection, or a third truth value. It is conditional on the axiom geometry.

The separately reported NRRF633 result adds a downstream uniqueness theorem: within an existing translational frame, a relational definition that is returning and grounded is uniquely the equality of returned identity. Translation then forces that equality to be natural across languages. This theorem does not originate the foundational translation; it shows what relational definition follows once the natural translational structure is present.

## Translational completion and closure

Translation alone is not completion. A forward relational path may exist without the returns required to recover its relational identity. The operative runtime therefore distinguishes raw translation/reach from explicit return completion.

The bounded completion-maze experiment tests the conjunction

```text
completion ∧ LocalIVI_W ∧ exact reach/equality alignment
```

as its executable closure proxy. Before return, raw reach does not realize the frozen equality classes. After explicit return lines, the completed reach relation does. The resulting saturation topology and receipt are derived only after this completion.

The broader closure thesis is therefore interpreted as **interactive continual completion** rather than a one-time equality test. A completed return can become part of the next basis, from which further frames and translations may form.

The separately reported NRRF599/639 results in the associated formal project are comparison targets for this runtime, not imported premises or locally audited runtime definitions in this checkout.

## IVI and nontrivial relational identity

IVI—intangibly verified information—is the informational interpretation of naturally recoverable relational identity without requiring unique reconstruction of a local occurrence. Distinct presentations may remain distinct while participating in one returned identity.

Thus closure does not erase perspective. It asks whether relative difference can participate in a completed relational identity. IVI supplies the nontrivial informational side of that thesis; translational completion supplies the returned structural side.

## Value and credit as relative agreement

The motivating application is broader than formal mathematics. Reasoning minds may carry different local definitions, measurements, or perspectives of value and credit. The thesis does not require one mind's definition to become the privileged global definition.

Instead, agreement can be sought through relative translation:

```text
local value frame A
      ↕ translation / return
local value frame B
```

What counts as shared value is then the relational identity recoverable through admissible translation, not arbitrary equality with one origin's local scalar. The bounded runtimes test the mathematical architecture of this distinction; they do not claim to have solved economic value or interpersonal agreement in general.

## 0 and infinity

The poles `0` and `∞` are not two absolutely isolated substances. They are relatively distinguishable orientations inside a closure relation. Their non-equality is presentation-relative; their closure equality is return-relative.

Thus the thesis is not ordinary arithmetic `0 = ∞`. It is that polar presentations may remain distinct occurrences while participating in the same returned relational identity. Reversal can change orientation without destroying closure when translation and return remain natural. This is an example of the broader principle that **distinction can survive inside relational identity**.

## Mathematical ASI

A mathematical superintelligence is a severe test of this foundation because it may generate axiom-geometries that were not selected by its verifier in advance and may continue changing them through reasoning.

The translational proposal does not ask such a system to remain permanently inside a human-selected geometry. A new frame is evaluated in its own unified axiom-geometric form; afterward, cross-frame translation is sought and tested for equality equivalence, downstream naturality, completion, and relational return.

Verification therefore becomes the recovery of relational identity across evolving mathematical frames rather than permanent allegiance to one static presentation.

The research question is consequently not only whether an ASI can produce a correct theorem in Lean. It is whether mathematical identity remains auditable as the intelligence changes the perspectives and axiom-geometries through which it reasons.

## Formal boundary

The Lean modules establish conditional theorems about explicitly specified translational frames, equalities, returns, and runtime realizations. They do not prove an unconditional proposition that all reality must instantiate one fixed `TransFrame`.

That is intentional. The metaphysical thesis itself rejects the demand for one privileged absolute frame. The formal programme instead asks how much identity, naturality, relational definition, completion, and verification can be recovered **conditionally across relative axiom-geometries through translation**.

The thesis can therefore be summarized as:

```text
origin-independent translation
    → relational formations / relative frames
    → local axiom-geometries and equalities
    → translation + return
    → interactive continual completion
    → IVI / recoverable relational identity
    → next perspective or axiometric basis.
```

The Lean results and executable runtimes test and constrain consequences of this thesis; they do not silently convert the metaphysical starting point into an unconditional theorem.
